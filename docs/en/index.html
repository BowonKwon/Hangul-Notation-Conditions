What Hangul writes and what it misses

A formal-condition table of 3,886 language units — exploratory edition 1 (November 2026)

When the sounds of a language are written in Hangul, some of its sound contrasts stay apart as different letters and others collapse into the same letter. This site is a table that counts, language by language and from public phonological data, where contrasts collapse and where the notation has no slot. What is counted is the sounds listed in those data, not every sound of the language.

The notation used as the fixed reference is Sejong notation, edition 3.2 (site in Korean): only the shapes on that site's closed list, with no new shapes, seven final consonants, and six tone slots written with side dots.

A row of the table is a language unit that the source data file under one code. Of the 3,886 rows, 3,843 are units that Glottolog classifies as languages, 42 are units that Glottolog classifies as groups of languages, and 1 is not linked to Glottolog (Method, "What a row is"). Below, "language" means one row of this table.

Before reading the table

Please read these points before reading the table. The details are on the Limits page.

  • It is not a recommendation to write any language in Hangul. Only the formal correspondence between letters and sounds is counted. Ease of reading and learning, whether speakers accept the notation, the scripts they already use, and national script policy are not measured. The course of the Hangul orthography actually used for Cia-Cia is set out on the Sejong notation site (in Korean).
  • It is an abstract condition check. The syllable condition lets up to about 5% of the syllables in a wordlist fall outside the frame (at the 0.95 cutoff). The tone condition looks only at whether the number of tones is six or fewer, and languages with unconfirmed tone pass without the number being known. Whether the sounds can be recovered by reading the written strings back was not tested.
  • It is an exploratory edition made after a planned test failed. Fourteen predictions about six well-known languages were written down before any computation. Seven held in the first run and ten in the second, short of the stated bar of twelve, so those runs are not reported as results. This table was produced afterwards by fixing defects and changing how the outcome is described; every change is listed on the Record page.
  • What the data do not record is not in the table. For more than half of the languages shown as having no tone, the evidence is weak (T0? in the table). Vowel length and nasal vowels appear only where a source inventory records them.
  • It does not rank languages. A category name says which kind of condition applies, and nothing is ordered within or across categories.

Order in which a category is assigned. Rows that have tone but an unknown number of tones, or whose sources disagree across the boundary between six or fewer and seven or more (undetermined; T0? is not placed here and passes) → falls short of the widened frame or has seven or more tones (outside) → falls short of the current syllable frame (syllable) → runs into something in the letter layer (letters), otherwise current. A row that runs into several conditions gets only the first category: a syllable row may also have mergers in the letter layer.

θ (the syllable cutoff) is the threshold for saying that a language “meets the syllable condition”: the share of syllables in its wordlist that must fit the syllable frame. θ 0.95 means at least 95% of the syllables must fit (the share is compared after rounding to four decimals, and a share equal to θ meets the condition). 0.99 is a stricter cutoff and 0.90 a looser one.

How to read it. Each horizontal bar is the same 729 rows, and the width of a segment is the number of rows in that category. The middle bar (θ 0.95, bold) is the cutoff used on this site: from the left, current 48, letters 198, syllable 456, outside 10, undetermined 17. The bars above and below divide the same rows again with a looser and a stricter cutoff. Across these three cutoffs, the stricter the cutoff, the more rows run into the syllable frame (syllable) and the fewer are current: current is 64 at θ 0.90 and 24 at 0.99. A category names the kind of condition that applies; the order of the segments is not a grade or a ranking. The counts are in the table below.
