1. How far the data reach#

Rows with data, by layer

The table looks at three things (layers) for each language. The letter layer uses phoneme inventories (PHOIBLE), the syllable layer uses wordlists (Lexibank), and the tone layer uses the tone counts in the inventories together with the WALS tone classification. The number of rows with data differs from layer to layer.

Of the 729 languages with all three layers, 712 receive a determinate category. For the other 17, the number of tones is unknown (14) or differs across sources at the boundary (3). Only 18% of the 3,886 rows have a determinate category. In 81% of the table, values exist for one or two layers only.

The darkness and size of a dot show only whether data exist — not a category and not whether any condition is met. Of the 729 dark dots, 48 are in the current category. Only rows of this table are drawn, so an area without dots is not an area without languages. 2. Counts by category and the syllable cutoff#

Categories of the 729 rows with all three layers, by syllable cutoff

The 729 rows with data in all three layers, divided by the first condition they run into.

current (plain)letters (diagonal stripes)syllable (dots)outside (grid)Undetermined (tone) (vertical stripes)

What the categories mean

  • current — Meets the conditions of all three layers of the reference notation (Sejong notation, edition 3.2) in these data and measures. Rows with unconfirmed tone (T0?) are counted as passing
  • letters — Meets the syllable condition. In the letter layer, different sounds merge into one letter or some sounds have no letter (with several inventories, judged by the median); or the language has vowel length, nasality or phonation contrasts (recorded in at least one inventory)
  • syllable — Falls short of the current syllable frame; meets the widened frame (0–2 onset consonants + nucleus + 0–2 coda consonants, a hypothetical frame with no restriction on which consonants may close a syllable)
  • outside — Falls short of the widened frame, or has seven or more tones
  • Undetermined (tone) — Has tone but the number is unknown, or sources disagree across the boundary between six or fewer and seven or more. A weakly supported “no tone” (T0?) is not placed here; it is let through

Order in which a category is assigned. Rows that have tone but an unknown number of tones, or whose sources disagree across the boundary between six or fewer and seven or more (undetermined; T0? is not placed here and passes) → falls short of the widened frame or has seven or more tones (outside) → falls short of the current syllable frame (syllable) → runs into something in the letter layer (letters), otherwise current. A row that runs into several conditions gets only the first category: a syllable row may also have mergers in the letter layer.

θ (the syllable cutoff) is the threshold for saying that a language “meets the syllable condition”: the share of syllables in its wordlist that must fit the syllable frame. θ 0.95 means at least 95% of the syllables must fit (the share is compared after rounding to four decimals, and a share equal to θ meets the condition). 0.99 is a stricter cutoff and 0.90 a looser one.

How to read it. Each horizontal bar is the same 729 rows, and the width of a segment is the number of rows in that category. Segments differ by pattern as well as color: current plain, letters diagonal stripes, syllable dots, outside grid, undetermined vertical stripes (as in the legend above). The middle bar (θ 0.95, bold) is the cutoff used on this site: from the left, current 48, letters 198, syllable 456, outside 10, undetermined 17. The bars above and below divide the same rows again with a looser and a stricter cutoff. Across these three cutoffs, the stricter the cutoff, the more rows run into the syllable frame (syllable) and the fewer are current: current is 64 at θ 0.90 and 24 at 0.99. A category names the kind of condition that applies; the order of the segments is not a grade or a ranking. The counts are in the table below.

3. The 712 languages with a determinate category#

Letter layer.

  • 97 languages meet the letter-layer condition (median K_c and K_v of 0 and no vowel feature). Five of these have a merger in at least one inventory.
  • K_c is 0 in 232 languages, K_v is 0 in 558, and 322 have no vowel feature.
  • Rows carrying each K_c type (a row can carry several): SUB 319, LAR 200, SAME 159, LAR+SUB 101, OTHER 33, CLICK 7, NOGLYPH 6.

Why the 198 letters languages are in that category. K_c is above 0 in 131 and K_v is above 0 in 45 (these overlap). By vowel feature: length only 68, nasality only 36, length and nasality 22, phonation involved 5, no feature 67.

Syllable layer. S1x (the widened frame) is 1 in 512 languages, below 0.99 in 46 and below 0.95 in 3. The widened frame admits the wordlists of almost every language, so in this sample it is mostly tone that separates syllable from outside.

Tone layer. T0? 244, T0 213, T1 168, T2 72, T1~T2 8, T3 7. One in three languages with a determinate category (244 of 712) has unconfirmed tone.

Flags. Single inventory 379, tone unconfirmed 244, fewer than 100 words 60, dialect data merged 49, tone evidence from dialect data only 6, hand-entered composite row 1.

4. The 48 current languages#

The names can be found by filtering the Table by category. Only the composition is given here.

  • Families: Austronesian 13, Atlantic-Congo 9, Nuclear Trans New Guinea 6, languages with no higher family in Glottolog (isolates and the like) 6, Uto-Aztecan 2, and 12 families with one each.
  • Tone: T0? 20, T0 15, T1 8, T2 5. Twenty have unconfirmed tone. Four of those are Atlantic-Congo languages, and of the 319 Atlantic-Congo languages in this table whose tone value is confirmed, 315 are tonal. "Unconfirmed" therefore does not mean "no tone".
  • Glottolog endangerment status: not endangered 23, shifting 14, threatened 8, extinct 2, nearly extinct 1.
  • 39 of the 48 rest on a single phoneme inventory, and 4 have fewer than 100 words.

current means "meets the conditions in these data and measures". It does not mean that every sound contrast of the language is written by this notation (Limits, section 2).
