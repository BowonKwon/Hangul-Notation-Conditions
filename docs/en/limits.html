Limits

What this table cannot tell you

1. Only form is measured

The table counts the correspondence between letters and sounds. It does not measure:

  • how easy the notation is to learn to read and write;
  • whether the speakers want it or accept it;
  • which scripts are already read and written, and which script schools and the state use;
  • whether fonts and input methods exist.

Meeting the conditions does not show that writing the language in Hangul would be suitable or beneficial, and it is not a recommendation to do so. The course of the Hangul orthography actually used for Cia-Cia is set out on the Sejong notation site (in Korean).

2. It is an abstract condition check

  • Syllables. current and letters mean that S1c is at least 0.95. Up to about 5% of the syllables in the wordlist may fall outside the frame (about 10% and 1% at cutoffs of 0.90 and 0.99).
  • Tone. Only the number of tones is checked against six. Languages with unconfirmed tone (T0?) pass without the number being known. The side dots are a device for pitch height. How contour tones, or tones that carry phonation, would be divided among six slots was not examined. The default Mandarin notation of Sejong notation edition 3.2 writes tone 1 and tone 4 with the same mark (one dot) and puts tone numbers in a separate column — so even with six or fewer tones, an actual assignment may not keep every tone apart.
  • Letters. K_c and K_v are the numbers of sounds that, in this classification, merge into a shared slot or have no slot. They are not a proof that adding that many letters would suffice. Whether sending a sound absent from the mapping table to "the nearest slot" is phonetically right was not separately verified.
  • Whether the sounds can be recovered by reading the written strings back (reversibility) was not tested.

3. What the data do not record is not in the table

Phoneme inventories are secondary data compiled from many researchers' descriptions, and they differ in what they record.

  • Vowel features. If no inventory records a contrast, the table shows no feature. A blank means "not in the data", not "absent".
  • Tone. T0? means neither "no tone" nor "six or fewer". Of the 712 languages with a determinate category, 244 are T0?. One example: Hainyaxo Bozo appears as T0?, but its grammar describes two lexical tone levels, high and low (with falling and rising contours on syllables), and contrasts of vowel length and nasality (Heath 2023: 7–8, 18).
  • Some things are left out by the computation itself. Diphthongs and non-syllabic vowel elements are dropped from the vowel layer (6% of vowel segments, in 254 languages). Fine place diacritics on consonants are ignored. These are in the source data but are not reflected in the table.
  • One inventory does not stand for a whole language. More than half of the languages with a determinate category (379 of 712) have only one.

4. The number of tones depends on the analysis

Sources count the tones of the same language differently. Vietnamese has inventories with six and with eight; Hausa with three and with four. The table gives the range as it is (tones_range) and assigns no category when the range crosses the six/seven boundary. Even where the counts agree, what is being counted as a tone may differ between inventories.

5. Dialect data are merged into language rows

Data that Glottolog classifies as dialects are raised to the row of their language. The inventories, the wordlist and the tone value of one row may therefore come from different varieties. 204 rows carry the dialect-merged flag.

Some cases escape the flag: those where a source dataset files a dialect inventory under the language-level code in the first place. The Dutch row comes out as tonal (T1) because the two inventories that record tone are from dialects (Hasselt and Maastricht), yet the row carries no flag. The absence of a flag does not mean that no dialect data are mixed in.

In the other direction, 42 rows are codes that Glottolog classifies as groups of languages to which the sources attach data, and one row is not linked to Glottolog (Method, "What a row is").

6. Wordlists are small and uneven

The syllable layer is counted from basic vocabulary lists. 217 languages have fewer than 100 words. Basic vocabulary does not represent the syllable frequencies of running text. The syllabification rules (Method, section 3) are also one choice among several.

7. The notation and the "widened frame" are choices

The closed list of shapes, seven finals and six tone slots are the choices of Sejong notation edition 3.2, and treating "two onset consonants, two coda consonants" as the widened frame is a choice made for this table. Other choices would change the categories. The widened frame admits almost every language in this sample (S1x is below 0.95 in 3 of the languages with a determinate category), so it discriminates weakly. It is also a notional frame that accepts any coda.

8. The counts are sensitive to the cutoff

Moving the syllable cutoff from 0.90 to 0.99 changes current from 64 to 24 and letters from 300 to 69. A category count is meaningful only when quoted together with the cutoff.

9. The Cia-Cia row

Cia-Cia is not in PHOIBLE, WALS or Lexibank and was entered by hand from the literature. Consonants and vowels are from Table 9 of Havens (2024), for the Bahari dialect; the words are from the ABVD list for the Wasambua dialect. The row does not measure the Sorawolio dialect, where the Hangul orthography was used. Tone is T0?: Havens (2024: 60) describes stress on the penultimate syllable but does not state that the language has no tone.

10. Languages the table does not reach

Of the 3,886 rows, 712 (18%) have a determinate category. Of the other 3,174 rows, 3,157 lack an inventory, a wordlist or a tone value, and 17 have all three layers but no category because of the number of tones. A language with no data is not thereby a language that fails the conditions.

11. A planned test failed

This table was produced after a test written down before computing had failed, and it was revised with results already seen (Record). Expectations were written down before each revision, but the possibility that the definitions were adjusted to fit the results cannot be excluded.
